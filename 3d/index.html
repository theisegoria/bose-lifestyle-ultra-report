How the motor works

Current in the voice coil, sitting in the radial field of the magnetic gap, feels a force along the axis: F = B·l·i. The coil is glued to the cone through the former, so the cone moves with it. The spider and surround are the springs that centre it and set its resonance; the mass of the cone and coil sets how fast it can be pushed. The plot shows one cycle of displacement.

Five ideal sources at the horn mouths. Focus 0 % feeds all five the same signal; 100 % delays each so its wave arrives in step along the steer direction and tapers the rear ones. The pattern sharpens with frequency because the ring is a larger fraction of a wavelength.

The up-firing driver and the forward tweeter, with their mirror images in the ceiling and floor. At the listener the ceiling copy arrives later than the direct sound; the two add at some frequencies and cancel at others, which is the comb in the plot. A higher ceiling makes the delay longer and the comb finer.

A rectangular room 4.2 by 5.0 m, an empty box with a table and a listener. Each wall mirrors the source; each mirror image is a delayed, attenuated copy. The echogram counts them at the listener. Absorption, diffraction, furniture and the speakers’ own directivity are not modelled.

Control around the cabinet.

A ring of tweeters can be steered: delay the ones at the back and the wavefronts line up in one direction. The Field chapter computes that beam from the geometry, and lets you turn it.

Control through reflection.

A driver pointed at the ceiling adds a second, later copy of the sound at the listener. The Field chapter computes the two together and shows the comb filter that results.

Sources & model boundaries

What the model can show.

This companion extends the published technical investigation. The two cabinets follow the published envelopes and the visible arrangement of drivers in the manufacturers’ cutaways. Everything inside them is a generic reconstruction of how such a speaker is built, drawn in Blender from first principles (cone, surround, spider, voice coil, magnet and plates, horns, ducts, boards), so that the mechanism can be taken apart and explained. It is not either company’s design, and dimensions inside the cabinets are not measurements.

The sound fields are linear acoustics of ideal point sources: monopole sums with delay-and-sum steering for the tweeter ring, and Allen and Berkley image sources for the room and the ceiling bounce, at 343 m/s. They model interference and geometry exactly for those idealised sources and nothing else: no driver directivity beyond a simple cardioid weighting, no diffraction, no absorption spectra, no room modes, and none of either manufacturer’s processing. The functions are tested in Node; the tests are in the repository beside the page.

Bose’s FCC internal-photo exhibit is listed for release on 11 November 2026; the model therefore relies on the public marketing cutaway, not an inspected production unit.
